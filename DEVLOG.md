PROOF (termination and correctness of the teardown loop): Let F be the nodes already released and R the nodes reachable from `cur` via left/right links. Loop invariant at the top of each pass: (I1) R is a binary tree rooted at `cur`, and R and F partition the original nodes; (I2) no left/right field in R points into F; (I3) R's in-order sequence is the original sequence minus F, and F is a *prefix* of it (nodes are freed in ascending key order); (I4) each node in F had its key freed once, `value_free` called once, and (heap mode) its node freed once, while nothing in R has; (I5) `t`, `value_free` and `pool` are live, while root/size/parent/color are stale and unread. A rotation keeps the node set and in-order sequence (`(A L β) cur C` = `A L (β cur C)`) and frees nothing. A free removes `cur`, which has no left child and is therefore R's minimum (so F stays a prefix) and is R's root (so nothing in R′ points to it); `next` was captured first. At exit `cur == NULL`, so R = ∅ and F is everything. **Measure:** Φ = 2N − S, with N = |R| and S = the length of the right spine from `cur`. Φ ≥ N ≥ 0 because S ≤ N. *Rotate:* N is unchanged and S grows by exactly 1 (the new spine is L, cur, then the old spine below cur), so Φ falls by exactly 1. *Free:* N falls by 1 and S falls by exactly 1 (the root was the head of the spine and the rest of the spine is `next`'s spine), so 2N falls by 2 and S by 1, and Φ falls by exactly 1. Hence the loop runs exactly Φ₀ = 2n − s₀ passes: n − s₀ rotations and n frees, linear time. Checked on an instrumented scratch copy of the real loop: iterations == 2n − s₀ with zero steps whose ΔΦ ≠ −1, for n = 1, 2, 7, 1000 and 10^5. Worked example on the perfect tree 4(2(1,3),6(5,7)), n = 7, s₀ = 3, Φ₀ = 11: rotate@4 (10), rotate@2 (9), free 1 (8), free 2 (7), rotate@4 (6), free 3 (5), free 4 (4), rotate@6 (3), free 5 (2), free 6 (1), free 7 (0). That is 4 rotations = n − s₀ and 7 frees, in ascending order.
